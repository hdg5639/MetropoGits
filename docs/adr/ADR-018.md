# ADR-018 Dependency road consolidation with bounded rerouting

- Status: Bounded opt-in parallel/short-junction consolidation and WebGL junction surfaces implemented; roundabouts pending
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Authority: maintainer request to document and implement shared urban roads

## Problem and objective

Nearby dependency roads remain separate even when a short diversion could reuse
another road. ADR-016's sequential sharing discount does not reconsider roads
after routing. Border styling alone cannot solve this structural problem.
Optimize the physical road network, not the sum of individually shortest routes.
Some route lengths may increase within a certified bound, while total paved
centerline and nearby parallel duplication decrease. Preserve every source,
destination, edge ID and complete path for selection and synthetic activity.

## First implementation

Keep UCG, City, districts, buildings, access ports and access spurs fixed. Take
the complete ADR-016 road graph and identify nearby parallel interior corridors.
Try removing a corridor, rerouting all affected dependencies on remaining roads
of the same kind. Commit the removal atomically only when every affected route
remains reachable within its original bound. Never use another building's access
spur as a through road. Remove orphaned pavement and rebuild segment provenance
and junctions from the final routes. No new road surface is created in this slice.

Let N be a route's original lattice-step count and M the Manhattan distance
between its fixed lattice ports. Its maximum is max(N, floor(100*M/90)).
Both terms are at most 100/90 times the true obstacle-aware shortest path under
ADR-016, so this conservative rule permits some diversions without weakening
the established bound or computing thousands of extra shortest-path searches.
The bound is frozen before optimization; repeated accepted changes cannot compound
the detour allowance. Door spurs are unchanged and excluded from the lattice bound.

Prefer removal of lightly used parallel corridors; deterministic geometry and
route-ID ordering break ties. Bound candidates and rerouting work explicitly.
If a candidate fails or the optimization budget is exhausted, keep the last safe
routes, not a disconnected or partially rerouted candidate. Record accepted and
rejected attempts, limits and before/after metrics. A partial initial routing
result is not eligible for a successful consolidation comparison.

Implementation bounds: at most 1,200 corridor attempts, 2,000,000 popped search
states across the pass, and 100,000 predecessor labels per individual search.
Inputs are capped at 100,000 atomic road edges and 1,000,000 route steps.
Search stores predecessor links rather than copying complete paths into the queue.
Up to two extra bends per route allow merging onto and leaving a shared corridor;
this allowance is frozen against the original route, like the distance bound.

The initial command is developer opt-in, not a change to CLI/Action defaults or
parcel scoring. It emits fresh viewer/preview/showcase assets sharing the same
`visual-access/0.3` routes. Canonical City remains byte-equivalent; geometry-derived
segment/junction IDs may change, while source IDs and route IDs remain unchanged.
Existing supplied 0.2 data remains readable. No input HTML/JS is executed.

## Junction surface rendering

### Road-surface legibility refinement

After structural shared-frontage consolidation, distinguish road pavement from
electrical-style linework without deleting any path. Use neutral asphalt, subdued
minor-road shoulders and grade-based dashed arterial center paint. Keep the
certified outer width, complete logical routes, quantitative grades and semantic
HTTP/messaging colors unchanged. Selected relation overlays remain continuous.

Dashed paint is globally phase-aligned on each straight world axis, independent
of segment subdivision or route count. Trim it at the same visible junction mouths
used by WebGL asphalt patches. Do not draw center paint on accesses/alleys or on
clearance-limited subpixel corridors; pavement itself remains visible/selectable.
Cap paint generation separately from geometry; a paint cap never drops a road.
CPU SVG, Canvas fallback and WebGL must agree on grade, palette and dash positions.
This is presentation only, not curved routing, a new connection or a roundabout.
Test segment reversal/splitting, clipping, semantic colors, exact route preservation
and Synthetic static-cache behavior; inspect actual same-camera outputs.

The WebGL viewer joins visible same-kind approaches at an existing junction by
insetting their rectangle union. Merged tiles fill the interior without extending
outside certified pavement or bridging an empty corner. Unlike the former small
square overlay, the patch covers internal shoulder seams. Normal centerlines stop
at approach mouths; short stretches between adjacent junctions can omit paint.
Selection's exact complete x-ray path and synthetic trajectories do not change.

Only visible segment membership participates; hidden branches and different
network kinds cannot create a patch. Geometry is built in the existing static
cache, not on synthetic ticks. This applies to supplied 0.2 and 0.3 routing and
changes no UCG, City, routes, IDs, building positions or clearance envelope.
Canvas/SVG already use unoutlined surfaces and remain unchanged. Their routes
remain identical to WebGL; this is not a new routing version.

## Short junction link consolidation

`scripts.road_consolidation --junctions` compares parallel-only routing against
an additional short-link pass. Start both from fresh complete 0.2 routing so the
distance and bend bounds remain frozen against the ORIGINAL paths, not the
already detoured 0.3 result. Do not feed a 0.3 result into a second optimization.
The initial parallel-only mode and default CLI/Action remain unchanged.

After the parallel pass, enumerate existing same-kind links of at most two
lattice steps (7 world units) between branch nodes. Internal nodes must have
degree two; all fixed route ports are excluded. Prefer fewer affected routes,
then shorter links and deterministic geometry. Revalidate live endpoint degrees
before each attempt: both must still branch and at least one must be a T, so a
successful removal eliminates a junction rather than merely changing four ways
to three. Reroute every affected source/target on remaining same-kind pavement;
commit only when all replacements satisfy the original distance/+2 bend bounds.
Remove orphaned roads and reconstruct exact provenance and intersections.

This transfers branching to existing surviving crossings; it does not snap
nodes together, move entrances, create a new connector, use access spurs as
through roads or make a new semantic relation. A rejected multi-route candidate
rolls back entirely. Additional pass limits are 600 attempts, 2M popped search
states and 100k labels/search; the existing input caps remain in force. The
comparison command computes the parallel baseline separately, so it performs
the parallel pass twice. Bound metadata is recorded in `junctionConsolidation`
within the derived 0.3 payload and decision report; public UCG/City are unchanged.

## Subsequent stages and acceptance

The short-link pass cannot remove a sole bridge or a link protected by a short
dependency's detour bound. Residual rectilinear topology is expected. Test
selective roundabouts only
where several principal approaches and sufficient obstacle-free space justify
them; update stored paths and animation together. Roundabouts are not decorative
substitutes for rerouting and are NOT delivered by this first slice.

Require exact endpoint/evidence coverage; no new road geometry or building
intersection; deterministic output; fixed buildings/ports; bounded detours;
non-increasing global paved centerline and parallel overlap; at most two extra
bends per route relative to its original path (a short merge/exit); and real
GJ/OJ before/after WebGL, static preview and synthetic checks.
Report changes in total route travel separately rather than rejecting all detours.
Unknown/sparse inputs must retain their safe fallback. Selective candidates and
bounded searches are not a globally optimal city road planner.
