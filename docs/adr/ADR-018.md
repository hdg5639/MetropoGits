# ADR-018 Dependency road consolidation with bounded rerouting

- Status: Implemented as a bounded opt-in pass; junction/roundabout stages pending
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Authority: maintainer request to document and implement shared urban roads

## Problem and objective

Nearby dependency roads remain separate even when a short diversion could reuse
another road. ADR-016's sequential sharing discount does not reconsider roads
after routing. Border styling alone cannot solve this structural problem.
Optimize the physical road network, not the sum of individually shortest routes.
Some route lengths may increase within a certified bound, while total paved
centerline and nearby parallel duplication decrease. Preserve every source,
destination, edge ID and complete path for selection and synthetic activity.

## First implementation

Keep UCG, City, districts, buildings, access ports and access spurs fixed. Take
the complete ADR-016 road graph and identify nearby parallel interior corridors.
Try removing a corridor, rerouting all affected dependencies on remaining roads
of the same kind. Commit the removal atomically only when every affected route
remains reachable within its original bound. Never use another building's access
spur as a through road. Remove orphaned pavement and rebuild segment provenance
and junctions from the final routes. No new road surface is created in this slice.

Let N be a route's original lattice-step count and M the Manhattan distance
between its fixed lattice ports. Its maximum is max(N, floor(100*M/90)).
Both terms are at most 100/90 times the true obstacle-aware shortest path under
ADR-016, so this conservative rule permits some diversions without weakening
the established bound or computing thousands of extra shortest-path searches.
The bound is frozen before optimization; repeated accepted changes cannot compound
the detour allowance. Door spurs are unchanged and excluded from the lattice bound.

Prefer removal of lightly used parallel corridors; deterministic geometry and
route-ID ordering break ties. Bound candidates and rerouting work explicitly.
If a candidate fails or the optimization budget is exhausted, keep the last safe
routes, not a disconnected or partially rerouted candidate. Record accepted and
rejected attempts, limits and before/after metrics. A partial initial routing
result is not eligible for a successful consolidation comparison.

Implementation bounds: at most 1,200 corridor attempts, 2,000,000 popped search
states across the pass, and 100,000 predecessor labels per individual search.
Inputs are capped at 100,000 atomic road edges and 1,000,000 route steps.
Search stores predecessor links rather than copying complete paths into the queue.
Up to two extra bends per route allow merging onto and leaving a shared corridor;
this allowance is frozen against the original route, like the distance bound.

The initial command is developer opt-in, not a change to CLI/Action defaults or
parcel scoring. It emits fresh viewer/preview/showcase assets sharing the same
`visual-access/0.3` routes. Canonical City remains byte-equivalent; geometry-derived
segment/junction IDs may change, while source IDs and route IDs remain unchanged.
Existing supplied 0.2 data remains readable. No input HTML/JS is executed.

## Subsequent stages and acceptance

After topology is genuinely shared, consolidate nearby approach junctions and
render each junction as a continuous surface. Test selective roundabouts only
where several principal approaches and sufficient obstacle-free space justify
them; update stored paths and animation together. Roundabouts are not decorative
substitutes for rerouting and are NOT delivered by this first slice.

Require exact endpoint/evidence coverage; no new road geometry or building
intersection; deterministic output; fixed buildings/ports; bounded detours;
non-increasing global paved centerline and parallel overlap; at most two extra
bends per route relative to its original path (a short merge/exit); and real
GJ/OJ before/after WebGL, static preview and synthetic checks.
Report changes in total route travel separately rather than rejecting all detours.
Unknown/sparse inputs must retain their safe fallback. Selective candidates and
bounded searches are not a globally optimal city road planner.
